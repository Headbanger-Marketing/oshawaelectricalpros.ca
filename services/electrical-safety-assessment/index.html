A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. Prepare the scope for your Oshawa property

Find any records from before the home was vacated, such as earlier ESA paperwork, invoices, inspection reports and the seller's disclosure. Write down how the household intends to use each room once it moves in.

Ask the contractor which equipment and areas the assessment will include, whether any testing is part of it, what cannot be reached or seen, and what written result you will receive.

What should the assessment establish?

What prompted the assessment is given in the report on a house in Oshawa that stood vacant, alongside the equipment and areas examined. Three separate lists cover what was visible, what was tested where testing was agreed, and what could not be reached. Any concerns are ranked, in the contractor's own words, by how soon each one needs attention. For each room, the report marks anything that bears on how the household plans to use it. No line of the report presents the visit as a complete approval of everything in the property.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does an assessment of a vacant home include every wire and device?

Usually not. Concealed wiring and blocked equipment can limit what is seen, so agree the areas and any testing beforehand. The written result should state those limits clearly.

Can an insurer ask for an assessment of a home that sat empty?

An insurer may ask for documents or an inspection, and requirements differ by policy. Ask the insurer what it needs in writing, then ask the contractor whether the assessment can be arranged to meet that request.
