Before work begins

Match the wiring to the new way the room is used.

Suppose the home is a bungalow or split-level with a finished or part-finished basement. A new bedroom, nursery or gym below grade can then mean cable routes through finished ceilings and walls, and a panel inside a room or behind storage.

Whatever the home, list how each room will be used once the change is made, such as sleeping, working, exercising or watching, because that decides where receptacles, lighting and dedicated circuits are needed. Photograph the panel door and its labels.

Should I tell the contractor how a room will be used?

Yes. The planned use shapes where receptacles, lighting and circuits are needed, so describe who will use the room, what equipment goes in it and what it replaces. A contractor can then assess the existing wiring against that use.

Can I keep the existing panel when I add a nursery or gym?

Sometimes the existing panel stays, but that is a judgement for a Licensed Electrical Contractor after assessing the planned loads and the panel's condition. Ask what, if anything, must change and why, in writing.

How do I compare quotes when the room plan is still changing?

Give every contractor the same written description of the room use and equipment, then check that each quote lists the same circuits, lighting points and receptacle positions. A gap in scope can explain a gap between quotes.

Who must be told before cables are run through finished walls?

Depending on the property and the change, a condominium corporation, a landlord or the municipality may need to know, and the electrical work itself normally needs an ESA notification. Ask the contractor which approvals apply to the scope.

What happens after I send my room plans through the form?

Your plans arrive as an enquiry and are reviewed. This information site does no work, arranges no visit or quote and confirms no contract. Separately, any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you go on to deal with introduces itself and shows its licence. You agree the scope, the cost and a schedule with that firm.
